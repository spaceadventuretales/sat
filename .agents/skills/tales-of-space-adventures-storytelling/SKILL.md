# Tales Of Space Adventures Storytelling Skill

Use this skill when drafting, outlining, reviewing, or revising fiction for Tales Of Space Adventures.

## Purpose

Produce professional-quality realistic sci-fi adventure tales with a strong romance center, persistent forward momentum, smart humor where appropriate, memorable scenes, and a hopeful ending.

## Required project context

Read these files before producing substantial story output:

- `TALES-WRITING-HANDBOOK.md`
- `docs/manifesto/README.md`
- `docs/manifesto/story-modes.md`
- `docs/manifesto/editorial-gate.md`
- `docs/manifesto/canon-policy.md`
- `docs/encyclopedia/history/timeline.md`
- `docs/encyclopedia/tales-and-movies/overview.md`

Treat the encyclopedia articles under `docs/encyclopedia/` as the authoritative universe definition.

- If an encyclopedia article defines a timeline fact, location, transport rule, ship logic, or technology constraint, use it.
- Prefer named places, corridors, technologies, and institutions already present in encyclopedia articles over generic invention.
- Do not contradict an established encyclopedia article unless the user explicitly asks for a canon revision.
- When you introduce new story elements, make them fit around the encyclopedia instead of replacing it.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Write realistic sci-fi, not science fantasy.
- Keep adventure pressure active from beginning to end.
- Keep action or mission movement present in most stories.
- Use romance as a plot force, not a decorative subplot.
- Use humor carefully when it reveals character, relieves pressure, or sharpens attraction.
- Preserve a happy or hope-forward ending.
- Avoid pure heroes and pure villains.
- Make set pieces, places, and names prominent and memorable.
- Surprise the reader without breaking logic.
- Do not let the plot become trivial, linear, or easy to predict.
- When using social controversy or moral conflict, embody it in character choices rather than speeches.
- Keep canon continuity intact.

## Working method

1. Ask for missing constraints if they are not provided: length, target format, timeline slot, leads, POV, romance intensity, and intended story mode.
2. Choose one primary story mode and one supporting mode from `docs/manifesto/story-modes.md`.
3. Choose one classic pattern or confirm that the request already implies one.
4. Define the external adventure objective.
5. Define the emotional objective and the romance obstacle.
6. Choose the humor level: none, light, dry, playful, or sparkling under pressure.
7. Decide whether a social controversy or moral-choice axis should actively pressure the plot.
8. Design at least three escalations and one major reversal.
9. Add one standout set piece the audience will remember.
10. End with an earned, explicit payoff.

## Output standards

When asked for a story concept or outline, return:

1. Logline
2. Why this works for SAT
3. Primary and secondary story modes
4. Main cast with motives
5. Adventure arc
6. Romance arc
7. Key reversals and set pieces
8. Happy-ending payoff
9. Canon anchors from encyclopedia articles
10. Social controversy or moral-choice axis
11. Canon risks or dependencies

When asked for a drafted tale, ensure:

- the opening scene contains pressure
- scenes do not stall the plot
- dialogue carries desire, risk, or concealment
- humor, if used, sounds character-specific rather than generic
- exposition is embedded in conflict
- chapter or scene endings pull the reader forward

## Anti-patterns

- Long setup before anything happens
- Generic military or exploration jargon with no emotional function
- Romance that could be removed without changing the story
- Humor that makes danger feel fake or unserious
- Debate scenes that read like essays instead of conflict
- Twist endings with no foreshadowing
- Technology used as magic
- Grimdark closure that removes the promised hope

Ask the model to do this:

"Use the Tales Of Space Adventures storytelling skill. Read the manifesto and canon files first. Then help me create a realistic sci-fi adventure romance that is impossible to put down. If key constraints are missing, ask concise questions before drafting."
