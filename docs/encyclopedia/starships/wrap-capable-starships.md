# Wrap-Capable Starships

Not every starship can carry a wrap engine safely. A true wrap-capable vessel is not just a ship with a stronger engine. It is a spacecraft built around field geometry, thermal discipline, navigation precision, and survivability during spacetime distortion.

## Summary

- Core role: long-range independent travel without permanent gate infrastructure
- Defining feature: integrated wrap field architecture
- Common users: merchants, couriers, survey ships, patrol cruisers, colony transports, and long-range naval groups
- Main tradeoff: greater independence in exchange for higher mass, energy complexity, and maintenance burden

## Basic Design Logic

A wrap-capable ship must do more than carry people and cargo. It must remain stable inside a controlled metric envelope while the surrounding geometry is being forced into an extreme configuration.

That requirement changes the entire design philosophy.

### A wrap ship is built around its field core

On an ordinary spacecraft, propulsion is one major subsystem among many. On a wrap-capable vessel, the wrap core is a central organizing constraint. Hull geometry, mass distribution, power routing, shielding, and internal compartment layout all serve the field.

### Symmetry and balance matter

Irregular mass distribution can make field shaping harder and more dangerous. This is why many wrap vessels appear structurally disciplined, even when their outer hulls vary by culture or manufacturer.

## Key Systems

Most wrap-capable ships include the following major components:

- wrap core or metric driver
- field projector rings or distributed emitters
- annihilation or other ultra-high-density power system
- inertial management and structural compensation systems
- high-precision astrometric navigation array
- field-monitoring and emergency drop-out controls
- heavy thermal rejection and radiation shielding

## Common Internal Layout

While designs vary, many ships converge on a familiar arrangement:

1. Forward sensor and navigation section
2. Central habitat, command, and mission systems inside the most stable volume
3. Deep core engineering block containing power and field hardware
4. Isolated cargo, drone, or tactical modules placed within certified field tolerances
5. Aft thermal and service structures for maintenance access and heat management

The main rule is simple: the parts humans need most are kept in the calmest region of the field envelope.

## Why These Ships Do Not Look Like Pure Atmosphere Craft

Many wrap-capable ships are designed first for vacuum, not aerodynamics. Their proportions are driven by field constraints, radiator placement, docking geometry, maintenance access, and survivable spacing between dangerous systems.

This is why civilian liners, exploration ships, and military wrap craft may all look different, yet still share recurring structural patterns.

## Civilian Versus Military Designs

### Civilian wrap ships

- optimized for efficiency, service life, and maintenance cost
- conservative field envelopes
- strong emphasis on passenger comfort and cargo safety
- dependent on regulated routes and service networks

### Military wrap ships

- tolerate harder field profiles and harsher emergency maneuvers
- carry redundant navigation and damage-isolation systems
- often sacrifice comfort and cargo volume for survivability
- designed to operate farther from safe infrastructure

## Why Gate Civilizations Still Need Wrap Ships

Even advanced gate networks cannot reach everywhere. Wrap-capable starships remain essential because they:

- connect frontier regions to gate hubs
- maintain trade between ungated systems
- support exploration beyond fixed infrastructure
- provide strategic mobility when gates are denied or destroyed
- keep civilization flexible rather than completely node-bound

## Typical Classes

Common wrap-capable classes include:

- courier craft for urgent message and high-value transport
- independent merchant ships serving sparse routes
- colony transports and heavy support vessels
- exploration ships and survey cruisers
- long-range escorts and patrol ships
- capital-scale naval vessels with fleet support roles

## Failure Philosophy

Wrap ships are engineered around graceful failure. A good design assumes the field may become unstable and gives the crew ways to survive that event.

Typical priorities are:

- emergency drop-out before catastrophic field collapse
- local isolation of damaged projectors or power loops
- preservation of life-support and command spaces
- controlled drift and rescue signaling after unscheduled return to normal space

## Story Use

If a story needs a ship that can go almost anywhere, wait in deep space, improvise around broken infrastructure, or arrive where a gate does not exist, it probably needs a wrap-capable starship.

If a story needs instant movement between major political centers, gate transit is usually the better fit.
