# Planetary Reshaping And Terraforming

Ordinary terraforming changes a world. Planetary reshaping changes what kind of world it is allowed to become.

## Summary

- selects a large starting planet or proto-world
- removes or converts excess mass where needed
- moves the world into a favorable orbit
- tunes gravity, atmosphere, water distribution, mineral availability, and climate
- prepares the world for seeded flora and fauna

## Basic World-Building Sequence

In the Space Adventures universe, the most advanced world-construction programs usually work in broad phases:

1. survey the candidate planet and its star-system context
2. determine target gravity, day cycle, atmospheric chemistry, hydrology, and biosphere plan
3. use matter conversion and extreme energy systems to remove, rearrange, or repurpose excess mass
4. shift or stabilize the orbit into a habitable band
5. build oceans, atmosphere, and mineral cycles
6. seed microbial, botanical, and animal ecologies in controlled stages
7. hand long-term regulation to planetary scaffold systems

## Gravity And Size

The target world does not have to be exactly Earth-sized. It only has to fall within the tolerances suitable for Earth-like life. Some engineered worlds are slightly heavier, broader, or denser than Earth, provided climate, biosphere, and gravity remain biologically comfortable.

## Atmosphere And Oceans

Advanced fabrication lets engineers build:

- breathable air chemistry
- stable hydrological cycles
- mineral distribution fit for agriculture and ecology
- crust chemistry that supports long-term biosphere renewal

## Why Some Worlds Fail

No world-construction program is perfect.

- orbital placement may be politically interrupted
- ecological seeding may diverge from plan
- climate control may become unstable
- corporate or state sponsors may abandon long-duration oversight
- later regimes may repurpose worlds for experiments, themed societies, or controlled biomes

That is how the setting acquires medieval worlds, engineered wilderness worlds, failed colony planets, and places that resemble living museums or nightmare parks.
