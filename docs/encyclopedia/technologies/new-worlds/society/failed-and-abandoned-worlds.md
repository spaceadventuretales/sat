# Failed And Abandoned Worlds

Not every engineered planet became a stable paradise. Some failed during technical execution. Some were abandoned after sponsorship collapsed. Others were later repurposed by regimes, cults, research states, or entertainment systems.

## Common Failure Types

### Partial worlds

Worlds where atmosphere, hydrology, or biosphere regulation never fully stabilized.

### Divergent ecology worlds

Worlds where seeded life evolved away from plan, often accelerated by genetic engineering, uplift work, or deliberate experimentation.

### Historical theatre worlds

Planets intentionally or negligently left in rigid social themes, such as quasi-medieval societies, ideological reenactment states, or curated primitive cultures.

### Bio-park worlds

Worlds built around controlled fauna and flora spectacle that drifted toward something closer to planetary-scale experimental wilderness.

## Why They Matter

These worlds are where many of the setting's strangest cultures and ecosystems come from. They are also where the health and biotech technologies of the universe become most visibly entangled with planetary engineering.

## Relationship To Biotech

Once DNA design, uplift, and species creation are common, failed worlds stop being merely bad climate projects. They become living archives of ambition, negligence, and uncontrolled invention.
