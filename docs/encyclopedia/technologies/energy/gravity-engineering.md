# Gravity Engineering

Gravity engineering is the family of technologies that gives civilization practical control over effective weight, lift, inertia management, and large-scale load handling.

## Summary

- role: control gravitational interaction or its engineering equivalent
- uses: lift, stabilization, inertia management, megastructure support, vehicle mobility
- importance: the bridge between energy systems and advanced transport

## What It Is Not

Gravity engineering is not simply "antimatter goes up" or "antimatter falls upward." Real-world evidence points the other way: antimatter behaves gravitationally like ordinary matter. In this setting, antigravity is a separate field technology, not a side effect of antimatter itself.

## What It Does In The Setting

Advanced gravity-control systems can:

- reduce apparent mass loads
- stabilize very large moving structures
- allow atmospheric or low-orbit lift without aircraft-style wing dependence
- soften acceleration stress on vehicles and cargo
- provide controlled field gradients for machinery and transport

## Why It Matters

Without gravity engineering, many things in the setting become too bulky, too fuel-hungry, or too structurally impractical:

- flying heavy-lift cities
- ocean-ship-scale airborne cargo platforms
- low-energy personal and industrial air vehicles
- field-balanced megaconstruction

## Relationship To Travel Technology

Gravity engineering belongs in the energy section because it is a field-power technology first. But it is deeply connected to travel systems, because many vehicles depend on it.
