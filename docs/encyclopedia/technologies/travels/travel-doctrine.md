# Travel Times, Safety, And Strategy

The Space Adventures universe uses two transport systems for extreme distance: wrap engines for flexible movement and hole teleportation for fixed high-value corridors. This page defines how those systems shape everyday life, war, economics, and frontier development.

## Quick Rule Of Thumb

- Inside a star system: hours to days, using system drives and transfer craft
- Between nearby star systems by wrap: days to weeks
- Across major galactic sectors by wrap: weeks to months
- Through paired hole gates: effectively immediate once a transit slot is granted

These are narrative-scale norms, not hard mathematical limits. Exact durations depend on route quality, field stress, traffic control, and local gravity conditions.

## Typical Travel Ranges

### Local and regional travel

- Orbital movement: minutes to hours
- Planet-to-moon or moon-to-station travel: hours
- Inner system cargo movement: hours to days
- Outer system movement: days to weeks

### Wrap travel norms

- Neighboring colonies: 2 to 10 days
- Common trade routes: 1 to 4 weeks
- Difficult frontier routes: several weeks to a few months
- Deep exploratory routes: months, sometimes longer due to cautious navigation

### Gate travel norms

- Physical crossing time: seconds to minutes
- Queue, customs, and slot control: from minutes to many hours depending on traffic and security level

## Safety Rules

The setting works best when faster-than-light travel is powerful but disciplined. These rules keep it believable.

### Wrap safety rules

1. No routine wrap ignition inside populated atmosphere.
2. No wrap insertion through crowded orbital traffic layers.
3. Strong gravity wells reduce field efficiency and increase risk.
4. Every route requires updated astrometric validation.
5. Emergency drop-out is safer than overstressed field persistence.

### Gate safety rules

1. A gate never opens beyond certified tolerance.
2. Mass, velocity, and hull geometry must match gate envelope limits.
3. Two-end synchronization must be confirmed before transit.
4. Military lockdown overrides commercial scheduling.
5. Suspected aperture instability triggers mandatory abort.

## What Travelers Experience

Passengers on wrap ships usually experience a normal voyage with briefing periods, field transitions, route corrections, and occasional communication blackout phases. It feels like serious transportation rather than magic.

Passengers using gates experience the opposite. The crossing itself feels abrupt and procedural, almost bureaucratic, while the real travel time is hidden in queues, inspections, convoy timing, and destination-side processing.

## Economic Consequences

Transport infrastructure creates economic geography.

- Gate-linked systems dominate finance, administration, and high-value trade.
- Wrap-linked systems remain viable, but pay more for time, uncertainty, and fuel.
- Frontier economies grow around repair yards, fuel depots, rescue chains, and navigation specialists.
- Bulk cargo may still move slowly when cost matters more than speed.

This allows the universe to have both rich connected cores and rough, semi-isolated margins.

## Military Consequences

Travel technology determines strategy more than weapons do.

- Wrap fleets provide reach, maneuver, raiding, and surprise.
- Gate networks provide concentration of force, reinforcement, and command coherence.
- Destroying a gate can isolate a region faster than defeating its fleets.
- Controlling refuel, repair, and field-maintenance nodes matters as much as controlling territory.

In wartime, a fleet with excellent wrap ships but no secure logistics can still lose to a slower force with superior corridor control.

## Political Consequences

- Central governments are strongest where gate access is dense.
- Frontier governors and system leagues have more leverage where wrap dependency is high.
- Neutral hubs emerge where many wrap routes cross but no single gate authority dominates.
- Migration patterns follow the cheapest combination of wrap corridors and gate endpoints.

## Narrative Consequences

These travel rules help preserve stakes.

- Distance still matters.
- Rescue is possible, but not trivial.
- Reinforcements can arrive, but not always in time.
- Frontier communities can feel remote without being completely disconnected.
- Characters can travel far without relativistic age absurdities breaking social continuity.

## Recommended Canon Defaults

Use these defaults unless a story needs a special exception:

- Wrap is common, expensive, and sometimes risky.
- Gates are faster, rarer, and politically controlled.
- Time dilation is not a routine social problem for either system.
- Travel delays come from logistics, traffic, field limits, law, and danger, not from arbitrary plot fog.
