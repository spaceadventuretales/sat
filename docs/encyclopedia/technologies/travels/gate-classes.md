# Gate Classes

Not all hole-teleportation gates are equal. Their scale, throughput, tolerance, and political value vary enormously.

This page defines a reusable canon taxonomy so stories can refer to specific gate types instead of treating every gate as identical.

## Class I: Local Transfer Rings

- Purpose: limited system-scale transport, cargo shunts, station-to-station high-value transfer
- Capacity: small craft, containers, specialist missions
- Traits: compact, tightly regulated, relatively common in advanced systems
- Weakness: too small for large strategic shipping

These are the smallest practical gate systems and are often embedded in industrial or orbital logistics networks.

## Class II: Civilian Corridor Gates

- Purpose: routine interstellar passenger and cargo movement between developed systems
- Capacity: merchant hulls, liners, escorts, standardized freight trains
- Traits: the workhorse gate of prosperous regions
- Weakness: expensive to maintain and vulnerable to congestion

Most civilians who have ever used a gate have used a Class II installation.

## Class III: Strategic Junction Gates

- Purpose: connect major corridor intersections, capitals, and military concentration zones
- Capacity: heavy shipping, convoy chains, war logistics, emergency evacuation flows
- Traits: massive traffic-control ecosystems and strong defensive posture
- Weakness: loss or blockade has region-wide consequences

If a Class III gate fails, trade maps must be redrawn.

## Class IV: Fortress Gates

- Purpose: military projection, rapid reinforcement, and theater command mobility
- Capacity: fleet-grade warships and armored logistics streams
- Traits: hardened superstructure, layered defense, restricted access
- Weakness: politically provocative and expensive even by great-power standards

These gates are military facts as much as engineering facts.

## Class V: Foundational Megagates

- Purpose: civilization-defining trunk corridors, intergalactic bridges, or ancient mega-infrastructure
- Capacity: exceptional mass tolerance and network significance
- Traits: monumental scale, unique maintenance doctrine, often culturally iconic
- Weakness: few in number and impossible to replace quickly

Some Class V gates are so important that entire eras are dated by their opening, loss, or restoration.

## Special Variants

Beyond the five common classes, stories may also use variants such as:

- black-site gates for covert traffic
- relic gates of unknown origin
- emergency evacuation gates with narrow load rules
- unstable prototype gates under scientific control

## Operational Differences By Class

As class increases:

- throughput rises
- approach control becomes more militarized
- political importance rises sharply
- repair and certification become harder
- strategic dependence becomes more dangerous

## Recommended Story Use

- Use Class I and II for civilian life, commerce, and local worldbuilding.
- Use Class III for contested trade and regional politics.
- Use Class IV for war planning and major crises.
- Use Class V sparingly, for places that should feel historic and irreplaceable.
