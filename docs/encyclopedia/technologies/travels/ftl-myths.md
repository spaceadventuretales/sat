# FTL Myths And Misconceptions

People living with advanced travel technology still misunderstand it. This page collects the most common myths about wrap engines, gate transit, and relativistic travel.

## Myth 1: A wrap ship is just a very fast normal ship

Wrong. A wrap ship does not solve distance by pushing a hull faster and faster through ordinary space. It changes the geometry of the route. That is why wrap-capable ships have such different structural and power requirements.

## Myth 2: Wrap travel makes time dilation irrelevant because relativity is bypassed

Not exactly. Wrap travel avoids the worst civilizational time-drift problem of ordinary near-light flight, but it does not mean relativity stops existing. Entry, exit, gravity environment, and field profile still matter. The point is not that physics is ignored. The point is that the ship avoids the specific trap of spending the entire voyage at ordinary relativistic velocity.

## Myth 3: Gates are basically magic doors that can be built anywhere

Wrong. Hole teleportation is more infrastructure-bound than wrap travel, not less. Gates require rare sites, giant capital investment, long-term stabilization, and strong political control. If a setting can place them anywhere casually, it destroys too much of the transport logic.

## Myth 4: Once a civilization has gates, wrap engines become obsolete

Wrong. Gates make hubs stronger. Wrap engines keep the rest of civilization alive. Without wrap-capable ships, frontier systems, military patrols, exploration missions, and off-corridor trade would collapse.

## Myth 5: Instant travel means distance no longer matters

Wrong. Distance still matters because infrastructure, law, queue time, risk, maintenance, and chokepoints still matter. Transport technology changes the shape of distance; it does not erase geography.

## Myth 6: Every delay in FTL travel should be explained by broken physics

Wrong. In a believable mature transport civilization, many delays are mundane: traffic windows, inspections, convoy assembly, route closures, field certification, customs holds, insurance restrictions, or threat alerts.

## Myth 7: A stronger engine always means a better wrap ship

Wrong. Stability, control, thermal management, shielding, astrometrics, and graceful failure design matter as much as raw power. Some of the deadliest ships in history were powerful ships with poor field discipline.

## Myth 8: Dead zones are empty space with no story value

Wrong. Dead zones are where hidden history survives. They are blank spots only on maps made by people who cannot cross them safely.

## Best Reader Default

When in doubt, remember these three rules:

1. Wrap gives flexibility.
2. Gates give speed.
3. Infrastructure still decides power.
