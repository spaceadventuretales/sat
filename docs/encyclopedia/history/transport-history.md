# History Of Interstellar Transport

The history of civilization in Space Adventures is inseparable from the history of movement. Every transport breakthrough changed not only where people could go, but what kinds of societies could exist at all.

## Pre-Wrap Age

Before practical wrap travel, interstellar civilization expanded slowly and unevenly. Early colonies were isolated by distance, forced into local autonomy, and often culturally divergent from their founders.

Key characteristics:

- enormous effective separation between worlds
- high dependence on generational planning
- fragile political continuity across systems
- local invention outpacing central oversight

## First Metric Theory Era

The first great breakthrough was theoretical rather than industrial. Physicists and field engineers established that effective route-shortening through spacetime manipulation might be possible if enough energy and control could be concentrated.

This era did not create working drives yet, but it changed civilization's imagination. Faster-than-light travel stopped being mythology and became an engineering problem.

## Prototype Wrap Age

Early experimental wrap systems were unstable, energy-hungry, and terrifyingly expensive. Initial voyages were short, heavily instrumented, and often one-of-a-kind technical spectacles.

Common outcomes of the era:

- formation of elite navigation corps
- strong military and state sponsorship
- catastrophic test failures that shaped safety culture
- first proof that civilizational timescale travel might become practical

## Expansion Through Wrap

Once annihilation-scale power and stabilizing field hardware matured together, wrap ships ceased to be singular wonders and became operational assets.

This changed everything:

- nearby star systems became economically connected
- corridor-based trade emerged
- frontier settlement accelerated
- law, war, and migration adapted to mobile civilization

The galaxy became traversable, though never uniform.

## Gate Theory To Gate Reality

Hole teleportation began as a more ambitious branch of spacetime engineering. Where wrap engines changed route geometry for moving ships, gate engineers attempted to create permanent engineered shortcuts between prepared locations.

The first gate successes were narrow, temperamental, and politically explosive. They proved that permanent transport infrastructure could rival or exceed fleet mobility under the right conditions.

## Militarization Of Transit

Once states realized that transport chokepoints mattered as much as fleets, interstellar politics hardened.

This age saw:

- fortress gate construction
- corridor denial doctrine
- convoy wars
- transit-law codification
- the rise of strategic logistics as a decisive form of power

Entire conflicts were won not by destroying worlds, but by deciding who could still move between them.

## Civilianization And Regulation

Eventually both wrap travel and gate transit became normal parts of life in developed regions. That normality, however, was built on dense regulation.

Transport matured into a full civilizational system:

- customs regimes
- route licensing
- safety certification
- rescue networks
- migration channels
- commercial insurance doctrine

At this stage, interstellar movement became something ordinary people could plan their lives around.

## The Modern Transport Order

The present age is defined by a mixed mobility structure:

- gates bind the most important hubs
- wrap ships connect everywhere else that remains economically reachable
- frontier corridors stretch civilization outward
- dead zones remind everyone that distance was never fully defeated

This balance preserves both scale and story tension.
