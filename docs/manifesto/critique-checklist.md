# Story Critique Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing a SAT story draft as a professional critic, editor, or development reviewer.

The goal is not only to say whether the story works. The goal is to identify where it is strong, where it is weak, where it looks artificial, and what the next prompt should ask the model to improve.

## 1. Hook and attention test

- Does the opening create immediate pressure, intrigue, conflict, or desire?
- Does the first page earn the second page?
- Are there early signs of generic setup instead of dramatic necessity?

## 2. Adventure and action test

- Is action, movement, or mission pressure present through most of the story?
- Does the story physically go somewhere, operationally change, or enter new danger?
- Do action sequences alter the plot instead of merely decorating it?

## 3. Plot intelligence test

- Is the plot nontrivial?
- Are reversals earned rather than random?
- Are there competing motives or only one obvious line of conflict?
- Can an attentive reader predict everything too early?

## 4. Romance integration test

- Does the romance change decisions, stakes, or alliances?
- Is attraction shown through action, risk, restraint, conflict, or sacrifice?
- Could the romance be removed without changing the story? If yes, that is a failure.

## 5. Moral friction test

- Does the story contain a meaningful moral choice or social pressure axis when appropriate?
- Are opposing positions intelligent enough to provoke debate?
- Does the issue live inside character consequence rather than speechifying?

## 6. Character strength test

- Does each lead want something specific now?
- Are competence and vulnerability both visible?
- Does major opposition make sense from its own point of view?
- Do voices sound distinct?

## 7. World and canon test

- Does the story feel like it belongs specifically to SAT?
- Does it use canon anchors from the encyclopedia rather than generic sci-fi filler?
- Are technology, travel, institutions, and setting constraints believable and consistent?

## 8. Style and sentence test

- Is the prose concrete, controlled, and readable?
- Does the style serve the story rather than call attention to itself?
- Are scene endings and paragraph turns pulling the reader forward?

## 9. Humor and banter test

- If humor is present, does it reveal character or sharpen chemistry?
- Does humor arrive at the right pressure level?
- Does any joking deflate danger or emotional sincerity?

## 10. Ending and payoff test

- Is the ending hopeful?
- Is the final emotional resolution earned?
- Does the ending reward the story's promises?

## AI red-flag test

These are warning signs that often make a story feel machine-generated, shallow, or insufficiently edited:

- generic or interchangeable character voices
- over-explained emotions instead of dramatized emotions
- repetitive sentence rhythm or repetitive paragraph structure
- abstract adjectives where concrete detail is needed
- tidy thematic statements that sound like summaries instead of lived scenes
- conflict that resolves too cleanly or too quickly
- false depth created by vague grand language
- generic names, places, institutions, or technologies
- exposition blocks that sound like setting notes pasted into prose
- "balanced" debate where nobody risks anything real
- twists that are visible too early because the setup is too neat
- scenes that all feel equally toned and equally paced
- dialogue that sounds polished but not personal

## Severity guide

- Critical: breaks the story's core promise or causes reader disengagement
- Major: substantially weakens immersion, momentum, emotion, or originality
- Moderate: noticeable craft problem that should be revised
- Minor: polish issue or optional strengthening opportunity

## Report expectation

A good critique should produce:

1. strengths worth preserving
2. concrete problems ranked by severity
3. AI red flags with examples or patterns
4. actionable revision directions
5. a ready-to-use follow-up prompt for the next revision pass
