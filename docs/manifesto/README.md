# Tales Of Space Adventures Manifesto

This section defines how Tales Of Space Adventures should be written, reviewed, and expanded.

Live page route: /manifesto
Current route source: src/pages/manifesto.mdx

Use this folder as the deeper editorial reference behind that public page.

## Mission

Create realistic sci-fi adventure tales and movies that feel impossible to abandon midway.

The emotional center of the project is usually love and romance, but romance must travel through danger, motion, sacrifice, discovery, and earned hope.

## Core commitments

- Realistic sci-fi only. No magic, prophecy, supernatural rescue, or invincible superheroes.
- Adventure first. Every story must keep forward motion and active tension from opening to ending.
- Romance with momentum. Love is not a pause from the plot; it must intensify the plot.
- Happy ending. The road may wound, divide, or mislead characters, but the final emotional direction is hopeful.
- No absolute heroes or villains. Readers should understand why each side believes it is right.
- Memorable presence. Characters, worlds, events, machines, and scenes must leave sharp mental images.
- Unpredictable, but earned. Twists must surprise the reader without breaking logic.
- Moral friction matters. The best tales pressure readers to argue with the characters and with themselves.
- Canon discipline. New tales must respect timeline, established technology, and prior events unless a change is explicitly documented.

## What makes a SAT tale work

A strong SAT story usually combines all of the following:

- Immediate narrative pull in the first page or first scene.
- A concrete external objective with real cost for failure.
- A romance pivot that creates harder choices instead of easier answers.
- Escalation every few scenes through discovery, reversal, pursuit, secrecy, or moral pressure.
- A debatable social or ethical tension when the material can carry it.
- Technical and social realism inside the universe rules.
- At least one prominent set piece that the reader will remember days later.
- A closing payoff that feels both surprising and inevitable.

## What SAT avoids

- Slow warm-up chapters with no conflict.
- Exposition dumps detached from character decisions.
- Flat good-versus-evil morality.
- Plot armor that removes risk.
- Generic planets, ships, factions, or love interests.
- Random twists with no foreshadowing.
- Bleak endings that deny emotional payoff.

## Required story questions

Before drafting, answer these questions:

1. What does each lead want right now, not eventually?
2. Why is this objective dangerous to pursue today?
3. How does the romance make the mission harder?
4. What hidden truth could reverse the reader's assumptions?
5. Which location, technology, or event will become the unforgettable set piece?
6. Why is the ending happy without feeling cheap?

## Editorial principle

If a story must choose between being merely clever and being deeply gripping, choose gripping.

If it must choose between fast spectacle and believable consequences, choose believable consequences.

If it must choose between cynicism and hope, choose hope earned through struggle.
