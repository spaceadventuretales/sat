# Editorial Gate

Use this checklist before calling a tale ready for publication or adaptation.

## Hook gate

- The opening contains active tension, danger, conflict, secrecy, urgency, or irresistible curiosity.
- The reader understands what matters in the first scene.
- Exposition is attached to action or decision.

## Momentum gate

- Every scene changes the situation.
- No scene can be removed without causing damage to clarity, escalation, or emotion.
- At least one pressure element is active at all times: time, pursuit, concealment, risk, separation, duty, or survival.

## Romance gate

- The relationship affects choices, not only feelings.
- Attraction is visible through action, restraint, sacrifice, or risk.
- The romance does not pause the adventure. It intensifies it.

## Realism gate

- Technology has rules and limitations.
- Travel, communication, injury, resources, and institutions behave plausibly inside the universe.
- No outcome depends on unexplained miracle behavior.

## Character gate

- Each lead wants something specific right now.
- Major opposition is understandable from its own point of view.
- Competence and vulnerability both appear on the page.

## Memorability gate

- The tale contains at least one highly visual, prominent scene.
- At least one location, device, or event could be recalled in one vivid sentence.
- Proper nouns and design details are distinctive rather than generic.

## Surprise gate

- There is at least one twist or reversal.
- The twist is prepared by earlier clues.
- The twist changes decisions, not only information.

## Ending gate

- The ending is hopeful.
- The final resolution is earned by choices and consequences.
- Emotional payoff arrives on the page instead of being implied offstage.

## Canon gate

- Timeline placement is known.
- References to worlds, factions, technologies, and prior events are consistent.
- If canon changes, the change is documented with rationale.
