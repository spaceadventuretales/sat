# Natural Human Prose Guide

Use this guide for SAT articles, encyclopedia updates, news posts, story concepts, dialogue, and fiction drafts.

The goal is not to imitate a particular writer. It is to sound like a competent, observant human who knows the subject and is speaking plainly. AI tells usually come from sameness, false polish, and overworked explanation, not from one forbidden word.

## Voice and readability

- Use clear, everyday words. Prefer `ship`, `road`, `alarm`, `promise`, or `fear` over ornate substitutes.
- Use concrete details and specific actions. Let one real detail do more work than a list of abstract qualities.
- Vary sentence length and shape. Short sentences can land. Long sentences can carry motion.
- Vary paragraph length. Do not give every thought the same four-sentence structure.
- Prefer the active voice, except when the passive construction is more natural or the actor is genuinely unknown.
- Use contractions in speech and a natural modern narrative voice when they fit the character or article.
- Keep transitions brief. A reader should see why the next paragraph follows.
- Let a character's education, age, job, mood, and loyalties appear in how they speak.
- Let an article's voice belong to a clear audience and purpose. It should inform or move, not perform intelligence.

## Concrete rather than summary

Name what can be seen, heard, done, or decided.

Prefer:

- `Mara closed the hatch and left the emergency light on.`

Over:

- `Mara felt deeply conflicted in a moment that showed the complexity of duty and love.`

Prefer:

- `The gate closes at midnight. One ship has forty minutes left.`

Over:

- `A series of mounting tensions created a complicated deadline with far-reaching implications.`

Do not explain a feeling after the reader has already seen it. Do not explain a joke after the reader understands it.

## Article and news style

- Start with the news, fact, question, or change that matters.
- Say who is affected and what changes.
- Keep technical terms only when they carry meaning. Explain unfamiliar terms once, briefly.
- Use headings for real topic changes, not to repeat the introduction.
- Remove throat-clearing, previews of the whole article, repeated restatements, and generic conclusions.
- Quoted or formal material should sound as natural as the rest of the document.
- End with the concrete next step, consequence, or open question. Do not manufacture drama.

## Story and dialogue style

- Put the character in motion or in conflict early.
- Use dialogue to reveal one thing and conceal another.
- A line should have a purpose: desire, refusal, deception, pressure, or a change of plan.
- Avoid dialogue that exists only to deliver lore.
- Use gestures and body language sparingly. One precise action is enough.
- Do not turn every emotion into a body reaction.
- Do not use weather, silence, or an object as a forced symbol in every scene.
- Keep interior explanation short. Action and consequence should demonstrate character.
- Allow people to interrupt, dodge, misunderstand, hesitate, joke badly, or say less than they mean.
- Character-specific wit is better than constant clever lines.

## Standard phrases to avoid

These phrases are not always wrong. They are listed because they are often used by default when a more specific sentence would be stronger.

### Article and general prose phrases

Avoid routine use of:

- `In today's rapidly changing world`
- `In the modern era`
- `As we navigate`
- `As we explore`
- `The landscape`
- `A testament to`
- `A reminder that`
- `It is important to note`
- `It is worth noting`
- `At the end of the day`
- `At its core`
- `In conclusion`
- `Ultimately`
- `In a world`
- `In a rapidly evolving...`
- `The answer lies in`
- `This is not just X; it is Y`
- `More than just`
- `The key is`
- `Deep dive`
- `Unlock the potential`
- `Game-changing`
- `Cutting-edge`
- `Robust`
- `Seamless`
- `Leverage`
- `Paradigm`
- `Resonate`
- `Nuanced`
- `Multifaceted`
- `Complex tapestry`
- `A rich tapestry`
- `Symphony`
- `Dance of`
- `Beacon`
- `Realm`
- `Journey`
- `Delve`
- `Unfold`
- `Embark`
- `Forge`

- `Empower`
- `Transformative`
- `Thriving`
- `Vibrant`
- `Ever-evolving`
- `Groundbreaking`

### Story and romance phrases

Avoid routine use of:

- `In that moment`
- `In an instant`
- `Time seemed to`
- `For a moment`
- `Little did they know`
- `There was no turning back`
- `Nothing would ever be the same`
- `Changed forever`
- `The weight of`
- `Every fiber of`
- `A mixture of`
- `Could not help but`
- `Unable to...`
- `She met his gaze`
- `Their eyes met`
- `The silence stretched`
- `The silence spoke`
- `A charged silence`
- `An unspoken understanding`
- `Something flickered in their eyes`
- `A flicker of...`
- `A ghost of a smile`
- `A quiet smile`
- `A bittersweet smile`
- `Lips curved`
- `Brows knitted`
- `Heart raced`
- `Chest tightened`
- `Held her breath`
- `The breath she did not know she was holding`
- `A shiver ran down`
- `Pulse quickened`
- `Blood ran cold`
- `The world fell away`
- `The air shifted`
- `The tension in the room`
- `Standing on the precipice`
- `Crossing a line`
- `Dangerously close`
- `The lines between`
- `A storm brewing`
- `Whispered` used repeatedly where `said` or a more specific verb is enough
- `Letting the words hang`
- `Hung heavy`
- `Something between them`
- `Fate`, `destiny`, or `the universe` as a cheap explanation
- `The two of them against the world`
- `Could feel the truth settle`

These lists are warning signs, not censoring rules. A phrase may remain if a character would naturally think or say it, but ordinary narration should not reach for it as a default.

## Construction habits that reveal a generated draft

Watch for:

- neat three-part lists when no list belongs in the scene
- repeated `X, Y, and Z` sentence endings
- several sentences beginning with `But`, `And`, `Yet`, or `However`
- contrast formulas such as `It was not X. It was Y.` used several times
- repeated `not only X, but Y`, `both X and Y`, or `X is not the destination; Y is`
- questions used as a substitute for transitions
- a problem followed by a perfect-sounding principle sentence
- one final broad lesson that replaces the concrete ending
- exposition in polished paragraphs that no character would naturally need
- too many evenly weighted paragraphs and uniformly calm sentence lengths
- emotion named before it is shown
- generic wonder words and grand stakes with no physical action
- interchangeable character voices
- safe, tidy conflict resolution

## Revision pass

Before delivery:

1. Read the piece aloud. Mark anything you would not say in a conversation or article.
2. Delete one repeated phrase, construction, or adjective family.
3. Replace summary with a sight, sound, decision, or consequence.
4. For every emotional line, verify that the scene has already earned it.
5. Cut one explanation of a joke, symbol, twist, or theme.
6. Check that dialogue matches the speaker's role, history, mood, and competence.
7. Vary sentence openings and lengths.
8. Remove filler transitions and generic closing statements.
9. Keep only details that affect action, character, mood, canon, or memory.
10. Read the first and last paragraphs again. Make sure they contain real material, not an announcement or a summary.
