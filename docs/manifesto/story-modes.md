# Story Modes And Narrative Skeletons

Use these modes to avoid sameness across tales while preserving SAT identity.

Each mode should still respect the manifesto: realistic sci-fi, strong adventure momentum, a romance pivot, complex morality, and a hopeful ending.

## 1. Quest Under Pressure

Use when characters must reach a place, person, device, or truth before a deadline.

- Best for: expedition tales, rescues, courier runs, political missions
- Romance pivot: two leads need each other to succeed but do not fully trust each other yet
- Main risk: turning the journey into repetitive travel scenes

## 2. Voyage And Return

Use when characters enter a strange environment, are transformed by it, and must return changed.

- Best for: unknown stations, frontier worlds, sealed habitats, deep-space anomalies explained by science
- Romance pivot: distance from ordinary life forces honesty faster than comfort would allow
- Main risk: wonder without plot pressure

## 3. Rival Pursuit

Use when two or more sides chase the same objective for different reasons.

- Best for: relic hunts, disputed discoveries, contested routes, extraction missions
- Romance pivot: lovers or future lovers begin on opposing teams or under conflicting contracts
- Main risk: reducing rivals to cartoon antagonists

## 4. Rescue Through Hazard

Use when saving one person exposes larger systems of danger, debt, law, or conspiracy.

- Best for: prison retrieval, medical evacuation, crash recovery, hostage exchange
- Romance pivot: the rescue target is emotionally central, or the rescuers fall for each other under pressure
- Main risk: emotional melodrama that outruns realism

## 5. Trickster Disruption

Use when a clever destabilizer forces rigid systems and guarded people to reveal themselves.

- Best for: smugglers, brokers, negotiators, infiltrators, charming liars with a conscience
- Romance pivot: attraction forms between someone who performs identities and someone who demands truth
- Main risk: cleverness replacing consequence

## 6. Forbidden Orbit

Use when love collides with law, faction, class, oath, family, or mission duty.

- Best for: inter-faction romance, command-chain tension, witness protection, diplomatic stories
- Romance pivot: the lovers are the pivot
- Main risk: relying on prohibition alone instead of building strong external adventure

## 7. Heist Across Systems

Use when the tale needs planning, role specialization, reversals, and layered deception.

- Best for: data theft, ship theft, extraction of a person, sabotage of a logistics network
- Romance pivot: intimacy grows through competence, pressure, and mutual risk
- Main risk: puzzle-box plotting with weak emotional stakes

## 8. Siege And Containment

Use when characters are trapped in a station, convoy, colony, or ship while options narrow.

- Best for: quarantine, blockade, mutiny pressure, failing habitat systems
- Romance pivot: closeness becomes unavoidable and secrets become unsustainable
- Main risk: static scenes with too much talking and too little change

## 9. Rebirth Through Expedition

Use when a damaged person or bond is rebuilt through a mission that demands growth.

- Best for: post-war recovery, second-chance crews, broken partnerships, survivors with history
- Romance pivot: healing and adventure develop together
- Main risk: introspection slowing the plot

## 10. Mystery With Acceleration

Use when each answer creates a more dangerous question.

- Best for: disappearances, hidden identities, sabotage chains, false historical records
- Romance pivot: attraction grows while trust remains fragile
- Main risk: foggy withholding that confuses instead of intrigues

## Using classic literature patterns

These modes are compatible with the classic skeletons often discussed in literature:

- Overcoming the monster: use as a system threat, not a magical evil being
- The quest: a natural fit for expedition and rescue plots
- Voyage and return: ideal for frontier sci-fi discovery arcs
- Comedy: use for collision, misunderstanding, and eventual union, not for lack of stakes
- Rebirth: useful for scarred leads and repaired relationships
- Tragedy: use its emotional gravity in the middle, but redirect the ending toward hope
- Rags to riches: use carefully through competence, access, reputation, or belonging rather than fairy-tale uplift

## Mode selection guide

Choose one primary mode and one secondary mode.

- If pace is falling, add Rival Pursuit or Rescue Through Hazard.
- If romance feels decorative, add Forbidden Orbit or Rebirth Through Expedition.
- If the story feels too linear, add Mystery With Acceleration or Trickster Disruption.
- If the world feels thin, add Voyage And Return.

## Minimum design requirement

Before drafting, declare:

1. Primary mode
2. Secondary mode
3. Adventure objective
4. Romance obstacle
5. Memorable set piece
6. Happy-ending payoff
